A while ago I saw someone who had written down a whole load of computer bugs they found within a week of just using software as they normally would.  I thought I'd give it a go myself, to see how much time I'm wasting constantly.

Monday:
- Dell support assist took the whole SSD for half an hour, making everything un-usably laggy.  I didn't even want it installed in the first place.  Various other shitty dell programs also caused noticeable lag.  I can't un-install them because its a work computer.
- Windows explorer stopped responding for a few minutes because I tried to re-name a file.
- The "menu" button to display a context menu from the keyboard doesn't work in adobe acrobat.

Tuesday:
- Outlook froze because I tried to open a draft email.
- Word started saving a document and set the cursor to the spinning/loading one.  It then never un-set that once the document had saved.
- Notepad spent a while showing 2 cursors in different locations, even though only one was being used (or even supported?).

Wednesday:
- Power button didn't turn on the computer despite lighting up.  Had to press it again.
- Adobe acrobat missed the space in "up to" in a dialog box.
- Spell check in word sometimes exits part way through the check, so I have to open the window again.

Thursday:
- Word's search pane stopped showing the list of results until I had closed all open documents and restarted word twice.
- Adobe acrobat's page rotate buttons next to individual pages are pixelated unless the mouse is hovering over them.

Friday:
- Outlook didn't draw a cursor if I had the to, cc or subject fields selected until I started typing.

Whelp.  That's about 2 hours over the course of the week.  Notably, none of the issues occurred on my Windows 10 computer at home, only on Windows 11, while at work.

At least I'm getting paid for the time?
